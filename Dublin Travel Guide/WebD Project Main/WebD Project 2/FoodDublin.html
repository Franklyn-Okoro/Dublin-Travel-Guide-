Foodness

Food in Dublin is constantly changing and there's a great deal of variety. While the underpinnings of the culture are quite Irish, the contemporary culture has adapted many things and the culinary options and creations abound for new experiences and offerings. If you're looking for an Irish staple or just a great brunch place, you'll find it here. Visit The Woollen Mills for a full Irish breakfast and step out onto the cobblestones.

Irish Food & Drink

Irish culinary creativity stems from locally sourced ingredients. Expect regionally caught fish from the Atlantic, root vegetables sourced from local farms, and farmhouse cheeses with updated preparation styles. Many restaurants employ sustainability options and farm-to-table offerings. For traditional comfort food, try boxty, coddle, and black pudding from Brooks Hotel Restaurant.

There are also several Michelin-starred establishments in Dublin that focus on elevated options for traditional meals, such as Chapter One and Patrick Guilbaud. Many places cater to an affordable budget yet provide comfort goods worthy of Michelin stars; for example, Leo Burdock's serves legit fish and chips and Bunsen does burgers the right way.
